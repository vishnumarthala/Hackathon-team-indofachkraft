# CareThread: complete role-play script (Stages 1 to 4)

The full multi-speaker audio script, from the parent's first call to the clinic through to the specialist handoff. **Synthetic role-play data only. No real patient information.**

| Stage | What happens | Product phase |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First-time approach | Parent calls the clinic for the first time and books an online slot | Before CareThread |
| 2. AI intake call | CareThread calls the parent that evening and builds the pre-session brief | Phase 1 |
| 3. Online session | Therapist interviews the parent, watches the child on camera and dictates scoring notes | Phase 2 |
| 4. Specialist handoff | Therapist hands the verified case packet to the specialist | Phase 3 |

## Cast and ElevenLabs voices

| Role | Voice |
|---|---|
| Aarav (3.5 years) | Non-speaking role, observed on camera |
| Parent (Ananya) | Warm, slightly anxious mother (Indian-English conversational voice) |
| Receptionist (Priya) | Polite, efficient front-desk clinic coordinator |
| CareThread AI | Calm, reassuring female AI assistant voice |
| Therapist (Dr. Sharma) | Empathetic, observant early intervention clinician |
| Specialist (Dr. Nair) | Authoritative, analytical developmental pediatrician |

Leave a 0.5-second pause between speaker turns so Scribe v2 can tell the speakers apart.

## Stage 1: First-Time Approach (parent calls the clinic and books)

*Context: The mother reaches out to the early intervention centre for the very first time after noticing developmental concerns.*

**Receptionist (Priya):** Good morning, Blossom Early Intervention Centre, Priya speaking. How may I help you today?

**Parent (Ananya):** Hello, good morning. I’m calling because I’m really worried about my three-and-a-half-year-old son, Aarav, and I’d like to book an assessment with a developmental therapist.

**Receptionist (Priya):** Of course, Ananya. I’d be happy to help you schedule that. Could you briefly tell me what made you reach out today?

**Parent (Ananya):** Well, he used to say a few words when he was two, and then he just stopped talking. He doesn’t look at us or answer when we call his name, and lately he’s been having really intense meltdowns. Our pediatrician suggested we get a proper autism and developmental evaluation done.

**Receptionist (Priya):** You’ve come to the right place, and we can definitely get him seen by Dr. Sharma. I have an online evaluation slot open tomorrow morning at 10:00 AM. Does that work for you?

**Parent (Ananya):** Yes, tomorrow at 10:00 AM works well. Do I need to fill out any long forms before then?

**Receptionist (Priya):** Actually, to save you paperwork, our AI assistant—CareThread—will give you a quick 10-minute call this evening. It will ask a few basic questions about Aarav’s history and prepare a summary brief so Dr. Sharma can spend the whole session focused on you and Aarav. Are you comfortable receiving that call around 6:00 PM?

**Parent (Ananya):** Oh, that sounds much easier than filling out forms. Yes, 6:00 PM is fine. Thank you so much.

**Receptionist (Priya):** You’re all set for tomorrow at 10:00 AM, Ananya. You’ll receive a calendar link shortly. Have a good day!


## Stage 2: AI Pre-Session Intake Call (product Phase 1)

*Context: That evening, CareThread calls the parent to collect structured case history and flag red flags before the clinician meets the family.*

**CareThread AI:** Hello Ananya. I am CareThread, an AI assistant calling on behalf of Dr. Sharma’s team at Blossom Early Intervention Centre ahead of Aarav’s first visit tomorrow. With your consent, I’d like to ask you a few questions for about ten minutes to prepare a pre-session brief for the doctor. Is now a good time, and do you consent to this intake call?

**Parent (Ananya):** Hello. Yes, now is a good time, and yes, I give my consent.

**CareThread AI:** Thank you, Ananya. Let’s start with pregnancy and early milestones. Were there any complications during birth, and when did Aarav first sit and walk?

**Parent (Ananya):** Pregnancy and delivery were normal. He sat up around seven months and started walking just after his first birthday, so physically he was right on time.

**CareThread AI:** That’s helpful to know. Now, tell me about his speech and communication. What words was he using earlier, and when did things change?

**Parent (Ananya):** Around age two, he used to say "Mama," "Dada," "ball," and "water." Then around two and a half, he completely stopped using those words. Now he just hums or repeats exact lines from his cartoons, without ever asking us for anything using words.

**CareThread AI:** I have noted that speech regression occurred around thirty months, along with scripted speech. How does he respond when you call his name or try to get his attention?

**Parent (Ananya):** He doesn't turn at all when we call his name—sometimes we thought he had a hearing issue, even though he hears the TV from the other room. He also avoids eye contact and doesn't point to show us things he wants.

**CareThread AI:** Understood. Next, I need to ask a standard safety question: does Aarav ever engage in any behavior that could hurt him, such as hitting himself, biting, or running into danger?

**Parent (Ananya):** Yes, actually. Whenever his routine changes—like if we take a different road to school—he screams and hits his own head with his palm until I hold his hands down.

**CareThread AI:** Thank you for sharing that, Ananya. I am flagging both the speech regression and the head-hitting behavior for Dr. Sharma’s immediate attention. Everything you’ve shared is marked as parent-reported in your pre-session brief. Do you have any questions before tomorrow’s session?

**Parent (Ananya):** No, that covers everything. Thank you.

**CareThread AI:** Wishing you a restful evening, Ananya. Dr. Sharma will see you and Aarav tomorrow at 10:00 AM.


## Stage 3: Live Online Assessment Session (product Phase 2)

*Context: The therapist reviews the AI brief, interviews the parent while observing the child on video, and dictates live scoring notes into CareThread.*

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** Good morning, Ananya! Welcome, and hi there, Aarav. Before we begin, I want to confirm you’re comfortable with our session being recorded so CareThread can transcribe our conversation and map your observations directly to our assessment form.

**Parent (Ananya):** Good morning, Doctor. Yes, I’m completely fine with the recording.

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** Wonderful. I reviewed the pre-session brief from your call yesterday, so you don’t have to repeat his birth history or the speech regression—I already have that noted. Today, I want to dig a bit deeper into his social play, sensory responses, and observe him while he plays there on the mat.

**Parent (Ananya):** Thank you, Doctor, that’s such a relief not having to start from scratch.

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** Let’s start with how he interacts with other children. Does he show interest when cousins or neighbors come over?

**Parent (Ananya):** Not at all. Last weekend my sister’s kids came over, and Aarav stayed in the corner completely alone. He doesn’t do any pretend play—he just lines up his toy cars in a strict straight line by color. If anyone moves even one car, he has a huge meltdown.

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** (Speaking clearly for the CareThread mic) *Note: marked lack of peer play, absence of pretend play, and rigid lining up of objects; rate item 14 as 5 and item 23 as 4.* (Returning to parent) I also see him covering his ears just now when the blender went off in the background. Tell me about his sensory reactions.

**Parent (Ananya):** Oh, every single morning! He covers his ears tightly at the mixer and the pressure cooker and runs under the dining table. At the same time, when he’s excited or watching the ceiling fan spin, he flaps his hands rapidly for minutes at a time.

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** (Speaking clearly for the CareThread mic) *Note: auditory hypersensitivity to household appliances and frequent hand-flapping stereotypy observed live; rate item 22 as 4 and item 30 as 4.* (Returning to parent) Let’s try a quick interaction on camera. Can you call his name from behind him without touching his shoulder, and then point toward that red ball?

**Parent (Ananya):** Sure. Aarav! Aarav, look! Look at the red ball! (Pause) See, Doctor, nothing—he doesn't turn his head or look where I’m pointing at all.

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** (Speaking clearly for the CareThread mic) *Note: child did not respond to name in three attempts during live observation and showed zero joint attention; rate item 5 as 4 and item 8 as 5.* (Returning to parent) Ananya, thank you for doing that. Between the live observations today and the history from yesterday, we have a very clear picture. Aarav’s assessment scores fall in the severe range, and because of the speech regression and self-injury when routines change, I recommend referring him to Dr. Nair, our developmental pediatrician, for a comprehensive specialist evaluation and immediate therapy plan. With your consent, I can send the entire CareThread case packet—including today’s video clips and scores—directly to Dr. Nair.

**Parent (Ananya):** Yes, please, Doctor. Knowing we don’t have to go through another exhausting intake from zero takes so much weight off my shoulders.


## Stage 4: Specialist Handoff and Case Review (product Phase 3)

*Context: The early intervention therapist hands off the verified referral packet to the developmental specialist, who queries the case memory before meeting the family.*

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** Hi Dr. Nair, thanks for taking a few minutes to review Aarav’s case. I’ve just pushed his CareThread referral packet to your dashboard.

**Specialist (Dr. Nair):** I have it open right here, Dr. Sharma. I see his total ISAA score is 162, placing him in the severe band, with two flagged red flags: speech regression at 30 months and self-injurious head-hitting.

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** Exactly. All 40 assessment items have been verified and confirmed on my end, with timestamped video evidence attached from this morning’s session.

**Specialist (Dr. Nair):** Let me quickly check the case timeline—I see the parent reported he had functional words like "Mama" and "ball" at 24 months before losing them, and your live observation confirmed zero response to name across three trials. Was there any history of seizures or motor regression alongside the speech loss?

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** No motor regression or seizures—his physical milestones were completely typical, which is documented in the Phase 1 intake log. Our primary focus right now is getting him started on intensive speech therapy, behavioral support for the self-injury, and your medical diagnostic confirmation.

**Specialist (Dr. Nair):** Having the intake transcript, scored ISAA items, and live observation clips already unified in one thread saves us at least two weeks of back-and-forth. Have my coordinator book Ananya and Aarav into my Thursday priority slot— we’re ready to begin treatment right away.

**Therapist (Dr. Sharma):** Will do. Thanks, Dr. Nair!

## ⚠️ Check the item numbers before recording Stage 3

The therapist's voice notes must use the same item numbers as `schemas/isaa.json`. If they don't, the form fills the wrong items.

| Voice note | Says | Item in our schema |
|---|---|---|
| No peer play, no pretend play | item 14 | 7 (solitary play) and 9 (no peer relationships). Item 14 is "excited or agitated for no reason". |
| Rigid lining up of objects | item 23 | 30 (insists on sameness). Item 23 is "pragmatics of communication". |
| Sensitivity to household appliances | item 22 | 31 (sensitive to sensory stimuli). Item 22 is "pronoun reversals". |
| Hand-flapping | item 30 | 24 (repetitive motor mannerisms) |
| No response to name | item 5 | 6 (responding to social cues) |
| Zero joint attention | item 8 | 16 (non-verbal gestures) |

Fix the numbers in the script, or check the official ISAA sheet and update the schema. The voice notes and the schema just need to match.
